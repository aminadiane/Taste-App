# Pallas literature research

Completed 2026-10-20 (America/New_York).

**20 additional books; 20 readable excerpt sources verified; 20 passages selected and analyzed.** None of the five excluded titles is included. All three requested files are saved. The website and GitHub repository were not altered.

## What the files contain

- **pallas-literature-research.csv:** one row per selected passage, with stable ID, bibliographic dates, NYT rank, source and metadata URLs, translation credit, exact short anchors, retrieval boundaries, all requested craft fields, and source-specific limitations. UTF-8 with BOM and quoted CSV fields.
- **pallas-literature-import.txt:** 20 blocks using the supplied `=== passage-id | Book Title ===` convention. Each contains an explicit retrieval placeholder and precise instructions.
- **pallas-literature-report.md:** scope, selection overview, method and remaining gaps.

All 20 full passages remain retrieval placeholders. None of the inspected sources established permission for us to redistribute the full selection in these files. Publicly readable excerpts are not, by themselves, a reuse license. Short textual anchors are included only to identify boundaries.

**Do not run the placeholder collection as a finished content import.** Replace each block's placeholder and retrieval instructions with appropriately usable passage text first. The delimiter matches the convention supplied in the request; importer field parsing could not be tested without accessing the existing code.

## Stylistic diversity

The selection spans six original languages: English, French, Korean, Russian, Norwegian Nynorsk and Spanish. Five passages are read in credited English translation: Ernaux / Alison L. Strayer, Han Kang / Deborah Smith, Alexievich / Bela Shayevich, Fosse / Damion Searls, and Melchor / Sophie Hughes. Observations about diction and syntax refer to those English versions.

The collection includes US Black historical fiction and nonfiction, Nigerian diasporic fiction, Korean American family chronicle, Canadian short fiction, British narrative experiments, French collective autobiography, Belarusian Russian-language testimony, Norwegian interior narration, and Mexican fiction. These are literary contexts, not claims that one writer represents a whole tradition.

Useful craft comparisons include:

- **Narrative access:** compare the withheld access of the husband in *The Vegetarian* with the blended consciousness in *Lincoln in the Bardo*.
- **Rhythm:** compare McCarthy's fragments with Fosse's recursive clauses and Melchor's accumulating approach.
- **Nonfiction form:** Wilkerson reconstructs a departure scene; Boo uses temporal framing and a labor montage; Alexievich presents edited testimony.
- **Objects and social perception:** furniture in Munro, hair and city smells in Adichie, and a wallet in Egan locate social judgments in practical details.
- **Self and collective memory:** Ernaux's image catalogue and Rankine's second-person vignette broaden the relation between individual experience and public life.
- **Comic registers:** Beatty's aggressive changes of register, Cusk's understatement, and Mitchell's period pastiche provide contrasting ways to build irony.

## Verification method and bibliography

The [requested NYT interactive](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2024/books/best-books-21st-century.html) could not be opened in this environment. Ranks were checked against the NYT-branded [printable checklist hosted by Northwestern University](https://english.northwestern.edu/documents/undergraduate/literature/nyt-best-100-books-21c-two-pager.pdf) and a [library-hosted ranked checklist](https://libraryaware.com/962/Posts/View/9f62894f-8951-4bcd-b6fe-8d81106fbfdd). The Northwestern PDF's extraction garbles some numbering on its second page; the library list resolves the ranks used here, including Secondhand Time 72, Septology 78 and Hurricane Season 82. These are the critics' list, not the separate readers' poll.

Excerpt pages were opened and their actual literary text inspected. Publisher preview text, including Fitzcarraldo's Read preview and Macmillan's Read Excerpt content, was inspected where returned in readable page content. Some consumer publisher pages exposed only a Read Sample button without readable passage text; those buttons were not counted as verification. In those cases, publisher education/retail pages or authorized reproductions supplied the readable passage. BookBrowse pages carry permission or copyright credits; Literary Hub, PEN America and the author's Station Eleven page also identify the work and source.

Publication years distinguish the original work, first English publication, the edition sampled and the web posting date. In particular:

- *Outline*: [Faber bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Outline.html?id=ClIaBAAAQBAJ) gives 2014; the excerpt site's January 2015 date describes the US publication.
- *The Years*: French original 2008, first English 2017 per [Seven Stories Press](https://sevenstories.com/books/4032-the-years), sampled UK edition 2018.
- *The Vegetarian*: Korean copyright 2007 and translation copyright 2015 appear in the Literary Hub source credit; the US edition followed in 2016.
- *Secondhand Time*: original 2013 and US English publication 2016 are supported by [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/541184/secondhand-time-by-svetlana-alexievich/); [Fitzcarraldo's book page](https://fitzcarraldoeditions.com/books/second-hand-time/) identifies the translator and UK edition.
- *Septology*: the [Nobel biobibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/2023/bio-bibliography/) dates the original volumes 2019, 2020 and 2021; the sampled omnibus page gives 2022. English volumes appeared across 2019–2021; national editions can differ. The original is Nynorsk.
- *Hurricane Season*: original Spanish novel 2017; [bibliographic cross-check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Season_(novel)); the publisher excerpt credits Hughes and gives the English publication as 2020.

## Remaining gaps and source handling

1. **Passage text:** all full-text retrievals remain outstanding in the import collection. No placeholder is represented as a completed excerpt transcription. Permission for Pallas's eventual display was not established by this research.
2. **Importer compatibility:** only the supplied block delimiter is known. No repository was inspected or changed, so the file is a retrieval collection rather than a tested automated import.
3. **Short selection:** *Outline* is deliberately limited to its three-sentence opening paragraph. It is useful for understatement but too brief to demonstrate the novel's complete conversational structure.
4. **Section naming:** the Station Eleven page does not label a chapter number. The Vegetarian source identifies the novel but supplies no chapter heading. Citizen's title is the publication's vignette label. These limitations are recorded instead of guessing chapter numbers or page numbers.
5. **Typography:** Lincoln's unusual spacing and voice attribution need comparison with a licensed edition before typesetting. BookBrowse's Goon Squad transcription has a later typo outside the chosen two paragraphs. Preserve Fosse's within-paragraph cut exactly.
6. **Source access:** the NYT interactive and some individual publisher URLs returned errors. Alternate readable authorized sources were used. A search result about a Believer excerpt related to 2666 proved to concern criticism about that novel; it was not used as literary text. The Macmillan Behind the Beautiful Forevers page was an adaptation by David Hare; Boo's authorized PEN prose was selected instead.
7. **Coverage limits:** this is a varied selection from the NYT's own pool, with substantial Anglophone representation. It contains one poetry/hybrid lyric book and no graphic memoir; it is not a survey of all global literary traditions.

## Suggested handling in Claude Code

Use the CSV's stable passage_id as the join key. Treat both status columns as meaningful: a source can be verified while passage text still requires retrieval. For each block, consult its linked source, retrieve only the bounded span when permitted, retain author and translation credit, remove placeholder instructions, and then validate against the existing importer. Preserve both original-publication and English-publication dates.
