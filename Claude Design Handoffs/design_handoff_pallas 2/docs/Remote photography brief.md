# Remote photography brief: Pallas, film screen (mobile)

Find or produce studio photographs of one real 1990s television remote control. They will become the remote that lies beneath the hyperrealistic silver CRT television on the Pallas film screen on mobile. The user presses the remote's real buttons to like or dislike film stills.

## The object

- A genuine handheld infrared TV remote from roughly 1992–2002, the kind sold with a Toshiba, Sony, Panasonic, JVC or Sharp CRT television or TV/VCR combo.
- **Shape:** long and slim, about 4.5–5.5 × 18–22 cm. A rounded top end, softly bevelled sides and a gently tapered or rounded bottom end. No futuristic curves and no universal-remote styling.
- **Colour:** charcoal, black or dark gunmetal plastic is preferred. Silver or grey that matches the silver Toshiba set is also good. Matte or satin finish, not glossy.
- **Buttons:**
  - **Essential:** a separate red power button near the top.
  - **Essential:** a large up/down channel rocker or a pair of CH+ / CH− buttons.
  - Desirable: a 0–9 number pad.
  - Desirable: a matching volume rocker beside the channel rocker.
  - Rubber or soft-touch grey buttons with printed numerals.
- **Printing:** small white or light grey screen-printed labels (POWER, CH, VOL, numerals). A manufacturer logo is acceptable but will be retouched out, so a small or plain logo is better.
- **Top edge:** a dark red or smoked infrared window.
- **Condition:** used but cared for. Light handling marks, slight wear on the most-pressed buttons, faint shine on the edges. No cracks, missing buttons, battery-cover gaps, stickers, dirt or yellowing.

## Photography

- **Background:** seamless pure white, to match the Pallas environment and the TV photograph. No props, hands, surfaces with texture, or reflections of other objects.
- **Lighting:** soft high-key studio light from above and slightly from the top-left, matching the TV photo. Gentle highlights along the plastic edges and button tops, with no hard specular hotspots.
- **Shadow:** a natural soft contact shadow beneath the remote, falling slightly down and to the right. The shadow must be on white and not cropped.

## Required shots, in priority order

1. **Straight-down overhead (most important).** The camera is directly above, the lens parallel to the remote face, the whole remote in frame with generous white margin. Shoot one version perfectly vertical and one rotated about 4° anticlockwise. All buttons must be in focus.
2. **Low three-quarter angle.** About 25–30° from the table, looking along the remote from its bottom end. This shows the thickness, side bevel and button height for depth.
3. **Close-up of the power button and channel rocker.** A macro of the rubber texture, printed labels and the gap around each button.
4. **Pressed state (if possible).** The same overhead frame as shot 1 with the power button pressed, then again with the channel rocker pressed. A finger can press from outside the frame, but no part of the hand should be visible.

## Technical

- At least 3000 px on the long edge. PNG or high-quality JPEG.
- sRGB, colour-accurate, sharp across the whole remote (f/8–f/11 or focus-stacked).
- No filters, grain, vignettes, dramatic colour grading or AI-added detail.
- **No generated or illustrated remotes.** These must be real photographs of a real object.
- Rights: royalty-free, licensed, or your own photographs.

## What will be done with them

- The background will be removed and the remote placed below the TV on the mobile film screen, slightly angled, with its real shadow.
- The manufacturer logo will be replaced with the small cobalt "cultural capital" mark used on the TV.
- The power button becomes **dislike** (the screen shows static, collapses to a line and then a dot) and the channel rocker becomes **like** (the picture swipes as if the channel changes). Small "dislike" and "like" labels are added beneath them.
- The pressed-state shots, if supplied, are used for the button press. Otherwise the press is simulated.

## Avoid

Universal or learning remotes, touchscreens, smart-TV remotes, remotes with big directional pads or menu wheels, glossy piano-black plastic, novelty shapes, and anything after about 2003.
